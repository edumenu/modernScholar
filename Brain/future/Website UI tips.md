### **Design Foundations**

- **Grid Systems:** Use a 12-column grid to maintain structure, but keep content within 1–3 columns for better readability.
    
- **Visual Hierarchy:** Use three levels of importance:

    -Level 1 (Large): Scannable headlines.

    -Level 2 (Medium): Supporting points.

    -Level 3 (Small): Deep-dive details for those who want to read everything.

- **Master White Space:** Use more negative space than you think you need; it helps the design "breathe".
    
- **Visual Hierarchy:** Use three distinct levels (Large/Hero, Medium/Supporting, Small/Details) to guide the user's attention.
    
- **Color Strategy:** Follow the **60-30-10 rule**: 60% dominant color, 30% brand color, and 10% accent/CTA color.
    
- **Reserved CTA Colors:** Use your accent color _only_ for buttons so users immediately recognize where to click.

- **Typography Rules:** Use "Display" fonts for short headlines and prioritize legibility for any text longer than 7–10 words.
    

### **Visuals & Media**

- **Subject Eye Direction:** Use photos where the subject is looking toward your headlines or buttons to subconsciously guide the user.
    
- **Technical Optimization:** Save images as **WebP** files and keep them around **300kb** to ensure fast loading times.
    
- **Intentional Cropping:** Use the **Rule of Thirds** when cropping photos rather than relying on automatic center-cropping.
    

### **User Experience (UX)**

- **The Fold:** Remember that only about 20% of users scroll past the top section; ensure your "Hero" section is crystal clear.
    
- **Reverse Scrolling:** Review your finished page by scrolling from bottom to top to catch visual inconsistencies you might have missed.
    
- **Action-Oriented Buttons:** Use specific text like "See Pricing" instead of vague phrases like "Learn More".

- **Real Device Testing:** Never trust a browser emulator; test on an actual phone to see how it truly feels.
    

### **Technical & SEO**

- **HTML Hierarchy:** Use exactly one **H1** tag per page to summarize the topic, and use **H2** tags for sub-sections.
    
- **Accessibility:** Don't rely on color alone to convey meaning (e.g., a green checkmark). Use icons or labels so colorblind users can navigate easily.

- **SEO Housekeeping:** Set up 301 redirects when changing URLs to preserve traffic and fix broken links immediately

- **Keyboard Navigation:** Ensure users can "Tab" through all links and buttons without a mouse

- **Dual-Cue Communication:** Never use color alone to show status (e.g., green for "Success"). Always add an icon or text label for colorblind accessibility

- **Website performance:** Use chrome dev tools to analyze website performance


### **Advanced Visual Design**

- **Anchor Font Strategy:** Use a tiered approach to typography. At the basic level, use single families or "Super Families" (fonts with serif, sans-serif, and mono versions designed together). For a pro move, combine families from different foundries. Choose your headline font first as the "anchor" to set brand personality (modern, technical, or warm), then find supporting fonts. Avoid fonts that are too similar (like Georgia and Times New Roman) — contrast is key. Use the website _Fonts in Use_ to see proven pairings for your anchor font.

- **Star of the Show:** A website shouldn't just be a "checkout machine"; it needs to make the user feel something to be memorable. Pick one dominant visual element — a specific font, gradient, or image — as the "seed" and build the rest of the site around it. Ensure the "star" relates to the business. For example, for a data-heavy software site, use abstract charts or vertical lines as the hero visual.

- **Visual Rhyming:** Create a cohesive "universe" for your website by repeating subtle details. Take elements from the logo (like triangles or curves) and use them as button overlays, icons, or image masks throughout the site. Rhyming can also apply to specific colors, textures, or gradients that reappear in different sections to strengthen the user experience.

- **Subtle Depth:** Make the site feel tangible and "real-world" rather than just pixels on a screen. Add light noise or grain to backgrounds or visual elements. Use "glassmorphism" (frosted glass effects) on cards and navigation bars to create layers. These elements should be present but should _not_ compete with the "Star of the Show" for the user's attention.

- **Master Opacity for Hierarchy:** Use varying levels of text opacity to guide the user's eye. Keep the most important headlines at 100% opacity. Drop subheadings or supporting text to 60%–70% opacity. This creates an immediate visual cue for what to read first, what to skim, and what to ignore.

- **Extreme Variation:** Borrowed from music production — break out of your initial thoughts to find the best version of a design. Don't just try to make your first version "better." Force yourself to try something completely different: create a light mode version, change the entire layout, or swap the "Star of the Show" entirely. Iterate through many versions before settling on a final result.
